\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Social accounting matrices underpin applied policy modelling, yet they are
built through undocumented spreadsheet work that cannot be audited or
reproduced. We introduce a construction-and-audit framework---every source
fingerprinted, every classification and formula machine-readable, every
balancing adjustment logged at the cell where it acts, and accounting
identities enforced as executable checks---and rebuild the 2019 South
African SAM from public sources and access-controlled microdata, auditing
it cell by cell against the UNU-WIDER benchmark. Its production and
commodity structure reproduces exactly, and its institutional macro
structure exactly except for one unpublished input (separately priced at
$+16.1$ per cent on import duties under its public proxy) and one
discrepancy consistent with an unrecorded adjustment. Kenya shows auditing
halting at the
boundary publication format sets; the Netherlands, generation and balancing
from three Eurostat API calls without a benchmark. We propose publishing
cell-level provenance and machine-readable construction rules alongside
every SAM.
\end{abstract}
